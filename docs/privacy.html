Privacy

Privacy policy

Last updated 28 October 2026. Applies to the Halftone app for Android (including Android Auto) and Linux, and to the website gethalftone.eu.

The short version

  • The app collects nothing. It talks only to the music server you sign in to. We, the makers of Halftone, never receive your music, your listening history, your login or anything else from the app.
  • No account, no ads, no analytics, no trackers in the app.
  • The website uses Google Analytics only if you click Allow, and loads its fonts from Google Fonts.

Who we are

Halftone is a free, open-source project, published under the GPL-3.0 licence by its developer (shadowfaxnl on GitHub), based in the Netherlands. For the website, the developer is the data controller under the GDPR (AVG). For questions about privacy, open an issue on GitHub.

All of the app's source code is public on GitHub, so you can check everything on this page for yourself.

The Halftone app

Where your data goes

Halftone is a client for a music server that you run or choose yourself, such as Navidrome. Everything the app sends goes directly from your device to that server, and nowhere else. That includes:

  • your username and a salted token derived from your password (the password itself is never sent);
  • requests to browse, search and stream your music, and to download it for offline listening;
  • changes you make to playlists and favourites;
  • play reports ("scrobbles"), unless you turn scrobbling off in Settings. Your server may forward these to Last.fm or ListenBrainz if you set that up there.

Whoever runs that server (usually you) is responsible for what it stores. Halftone has no server of its own.

On Android, your login and your downloads are excluded from device backups and from transfers to a new phone. Other settings may be included in your Google backup if you have backups turned on. On Linux, settings are stored in ~/.config/halftone/settings.json, readable only by your user account, and downloads in ~/.local/share/halftone.

Android Auto and your phone's system

To work like a music app should, Halftone shares the song that's playing, its cover and your library listings with your phone's own media controls: the notification, the lock screen, Bluetooth devices and, if you use it, Android Auto. Voice commands such as "Hey Google, play … on Halftone" are handled by Google Assistant under Google's privacy policy; Halftone only receives the search words and looks them up on your server. On Linux, the same information is shared with your desktop through MPRIS for media keys and controls.

Permissions

  • Internet and network state: to reach your server and notice when you're offline.
  • Notifications: for playback controls and download progress. You can decline; downloading still works.
  • Foreground service and wake lock: so music and downloads keep going when the screen is off.

Halftone does not ask for your location, contacts, camera, microphone or files.

Google Play

If you install Halftone from Google Play, Google handles the download and may share anonymous, aggregated crash and performance statistics with us if you've allowed your phone to share usage and diagnostics with app developers. This is governed by Google's privacy policy. Halftone itself contains no crash reporter.

Children

The app is a general-purpose music player and isn't directed at children. It collects no data from anyone, including children.

The website gethalftone.eu

Hosting

The site is hosted on GitHub Pages. GitHub may log technical data such as your IP address when it serves the pages, to keep the service secure. See GitHub's privacy statement.

Fonts

The home page loads its typefaces from Google Fonts. Your browser connects to Google's servers for this, which means Google receives your IP address. Google states it doesn't use this to build profiles. This page loads no external fonts.

Google Analytics, only with your consent

When you first visit, we ask whether we may count your visit. Only if you click Allow do we load Google Analytics, which then places cookies and tells us, without your name, things like how many people visited, which sections they read, roughly which country they came from, what kind of device they used, and how often the download buttons were clicked. We don't use it for advertising and we don't combine it with other data.

  • Legal basis: your consent (GDPR article 6(1)(a)).
  • Who receives it: Google Ireland Ltd., which may process it in the United States under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework.
  • How long: Google Analytics keeps the data for 2 months.
  • Changing your mind: use Cookie settings at the bottom of the home page at any time. Your choice is remembered in your browser's local storage under halftone-analytics; it isn't sent anywhere.

If you click No thanks, Google Analytics is never loaded and no analytics cookies are set.

Suggestions and bug reports

The suggestion form on the site doesn't send anything by itself. It opens a pre-filled issue on GitHub, which you post with your own GitHub account and which is public. GitHub's privacy statement applies there.

Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right to access, correct or delete personal data about you, to object to its use and to withdraw consent. Because the app collects nothing, we hold no app data about you. For the website, you can withdraw consent via Cookie settings, and ask us anything through GitHub. If you think we handle your data wrongly, you can complain to the Dutch data protection authority, the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens.

Changes

If Halftone starts handling data differently, we'll update this page and the date at the top before that version is released. The full history of this page is visible on GitHub.
